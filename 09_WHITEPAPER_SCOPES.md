# Dronect — Whitepaper Specifications (v0.2; not final papers)

The team requested two **English** whitepapers **only after material uncertainties are closed**. This file captures the detailed outlines and evidence gate now. Publishing full definitive documents before sensor field tests, site geometry, quotes, customer discovery and legal classification would falsely convert aspirations to findings.

## A. Technical whitepaper — planned

**Proposed title:** *Dronect: Evidence-Aware Multimodal Airspace Monitoring for R&D Enterprise Security — Technical Architecture and Validation*.

### Abstract intent

A research-led technical design for passive, integrated airspace awareness around high-value corporate sites. Clearly distinguish cooperative Remote ID reception, non-RID RF links and genuinely RF-silent autonomous craft. Present audio/visual/radio observations as imperfect complementary modalities, and articulate the operational path from event detection to evidence-aware response.

### Sections

1. Executive technical overview and intended operating environment.
2. Threat model: hostile observation vs authorised/benign air traffic, autonomy and noncooperative behaviour.
3. Taxonomy of detect / classify / localise / track / identify / assess risk.
4. Regulatory and privacy constraints imposed on system architecture.
5. Survey method and coverage geometry; exposed-asset-first deployment.
6. Hardware and sensor choice: RID, acoustic, EO, optional RF fingerprints and future radar (quotes and comparative tests required).
7. Edge/software design, event schema, modular device adapters and local-first resilience.
8. Algorithms, sensor fusion, confidence calibration, negative evidence and false-alarm control.
9. Security architecture, incident logging, privacy, access and evidence integrity.
10. Measurement methodology: autonomous/RF-silent trials, heterogeneous conditions, ground truth, confidence intervals and blind spots.
11. Actual results, errors and remediation roadmap — **must not be invented**.
12. Operations, maintenance, failure modes, supplier risk and production-hardening plan.
13. Findings, limitations and research roadmap.
14. References, technical BOM, test annex, diagrams and sample event schema.

### Technical release gate

Documented site geometry, selected/quoted sensors, legal test authorisation, reproducible experimental logs, independently reviewed technical claims, measured results with uncertainty, tested security design and explicit unsupported conditions. The 99.9% goal cannot appear as an achieved result absent appropriate testing.

## B. Business whitepaper — planned

**Proposed title:** *Dronect: A Managed Aerial-Security Service for European R&D Enterprises — Market Thesis, Service Operations and Unit Economics*.

### Abstract intent

A narrowly defined B2B service business that integrates survey, leased sensing, customised response design, training, evidence handoff and recurring incident coordination. Its near-term objective is an economically contained Spanish pilot; its eventual opportunity is larger installations and, if justified, privacy-preserving multi-client risk intelligence.

### Sections

1. Executive investment thesis and explicit status of hypotheses.
2. Problem and economic exposure: high-value visible trade secrets; when aerial observation is material.
3. Market map: EU R&D-intensive industries, prospective purchasing roles, buyer interviews and procurement restrictions.
4. Existing alternatives: Orange Business, Dedrone, Materna, other managed providers; feature/segment comparison without first-to-market claims.
5. Service blueprint: risk audit, acquisition/rental, onboarding, runbooks, training, triage, lawful response, reports and improvements.
6. Responsible legal operating model in Spain and expansion model within EU jurisdictions.
7. Customer acquisition and proof of willingness to pay; initial site-selection criteria and a pilot agreement framework.
8. Detailed six-month pilot cashflow: €1,200/quarter, self-performed labour, hardware US$2k aspiration, out-of-pocket reserve, VAT/collection assumptions and downside cases.
9. Shadow economics versus mature subscription price, staffing, insurance and operational leverage.
10. Implementation/risk roadmap, supplier lock-in, quality assurance and failure contingencies.
11. KPI definitions: site contribution, renewal, ARR, unit cost, response outcome, false alarm operational burden and referral conversion.
12. Trust/IP handling, confidentiality, client consent and data monetisation safeguards.
13. Scenario-based scaling; multi-site coverage not proportional to floor area; eventual optional B2B2G.
14. Risks, explicit no-go conditions and next financing decision.
15. Sources, verified interviews/quotes, appendices and assumptions register.

### Business release gate

Verified first ICP + buyer evidence; actual installed BOM; a compliant/insured service definition; signed/credible pilot terms; labour-shadow cost and full-service price scenario; downside and break-even model; no invented total addressable market, customer conversion or future defence revenue.
