# Dronect — Business Model & Pilot Unit Economics (v0.2)

**Decision change:** During MVP validation, founders' installation, audit, software development and incident-management time will not generate cash wage expense. The objective is **avoid negative six-month direct cash contribution**, not prove a sustainable gross margin. All figures below are **illustrative, not supplier quotations**.

## 1. Pilot commercial proposition

- **Customer:** European R&D-intensive enterprise, initially in Spain; one scoped building side / valuable exposed zone.
- **Offer:** hardware loan/rental, self-performed installation, risk assessment, tailored runbooks, training/handover, practical incident coordination and platform operation **in one contract**.
- **Target charge:** **€1,200 per three-month period**, excluding VAT, paid for two consecutive quarters. Revenue over six months: **€2,400** excluding VAT if contracted and collected.
- **Internal work:** founders unpaid during validation; record time at a realistic internal rate as a **shadow cost**.
- **Separately scoped rather than silently included:** blinds/automation equipment and labour; litigation, patent prosecution and extraordinary representation; full 24/7 licensed SOC; extraordinary site works, extra nodes, dedicated radar or specialist camera systems. **Basic legal-incident guidance remains within the integrated service**; partner counsel needed to deliver it must be priced into the pilot envelope, or provided through a documented customer-counsel arrangement.
- **Hardware reference cap:** **US$2,000 max** proposed by Gemini/team, for an initial sensor package, **not verified**. A US-dollar cap is not the same as a euro landing-cost cap: verify exchange, shipping, import duties/VAT, warranty and availability.

## 2. Six-month cash-only equation

```text
Pilot cash contribution = 2 × €1,200
                       − acquired equipment (cash landed cost)
                       − installation consumables / travel
                       − six-month external connectivity & hosting
                       − allocated customer-specific external charges
                       − other out-of-pocket cash expenses
```

The equation **excludes founder labour, depreciation after the pilot, customer acquisition, corporate overhead and taxes on profits** by design, and is thus **not** a full P&L margin.

### Illustrative 3-case model (all euro figures net of recoverable VAT)

| Pilot 6-month cash category | Lean prototype | Target integrated node | Stress / overrun |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Purchased sensor/compute/material kit | €1,250 | €1,610 | €2,420 |
| Customer-specific travel/consumables | €150 | €170 | €200 |
| Six-month external operations | €120 | €220 | €300 |
| **Total 6-month external cash costs** | **€1,520** | **€2,000** | **€2,920** |
| **Revenue ex VAT** | **€2,400** | **€2,400** | **€2,400** |
| **Cash contribution at month 6** | **+€880** | **+€400** | **−€520** |

Notes: these estimates assume any included basic legal-incident support is supplied by appropriately qualified team members or customer counsel without incremental cash invoices. **If a paid qualified external lawyer is needed, add the fee and retest breakeven; it is not silently free.** Cases are **engineering budget envelopes**, not supplier quotes or demonstrated viable technical configurations. Target case leaves only **€400** buffer; this can be eliminated by a faulty sensor, insurance charge, travel, cloud licence, significant shipping expense or replacement. If equipment is recoverable/redeployable after the contract, residual value may matter economically but should **not** be used to claim cash breakeven without an actual resale or reuse plan.

### VAT and collection qualification

- Revenue **€1,200 excluding VAT**/quarter is not the same as **€1,200 VAT included**. If Spanish 21% VAT applies and the marketed €1,200 *includes* VAT, six-month revenue **net of VAT** is **€1,983.47**, not €2,400; that lowers target-case contribution from +€400 to approximately **−€16.53** under equivalent ex-VAT costs.
- Vat collections and deductible input VAT affect actual cash timing. VAT treatment and B2B cross-border supply must be confirmed with an accountant; the model uses net-of-recoverable-VAT comparisons only.
- Cash-neutral over six months is different from having sufficient **working capital on day one**.

## 3. Suggested capital for **one** validation pilot

Do not start fundraising with a fictitious "€0 launch cost" because founders work free. Proposed **working-capital envelope €2,500–€4,000** for one site and a prototype/test period, assuming an inexpensive multimodal kit and no regulated third-party alarm monitoring. **This is a funding planning range**, not expected actual spending.

Illustrative financing uses:

| Use | Illustrative reserve |
|---|---:|
| Target kit | €1,610 |
| First-site setup/travel | €170 |
| Six-month out-of-pocket operations | €220 |
| Early tests, spares, unexpected replacement | €400–€800 |
| Timing/tax/quotation buffer | €100–€1,200 |
| **Financing envelope** | **€2,500–€4,000** |

Company formation, general liability/professional insurance, cyber insurance, upfront regulatory opinion, litigation/patent representation and formal certification are **not priced here**. If basic legal guidance must be delivered by a paid qualified partner, its hours and fee **must** be added to the site cash model (potentially destroying cash neutrality); do not promise it for free. Depending on whether these are mandatory before a pilot, they may push the *total enterprise startup budget* higher; no lawful deployment should proceed by ignoring a mandatory cost.

### Sample cash timing (net VAT, simplified)

If the customer pays €1,200 upfront for quarter one and €1,200 at start of quarter two:

- At deployment, €1,610 kit + €170 travel = €1,780; first invoice gives €1,200 → **€580 initial funding gap**.
- Over months 1–3, approx. €110 external operations increase the temporary gap to **€690**.
- Quarter-two payment closes that simplified gap, but any late payment or pre-pilot testing moves the financing peak materially.

A pilot is cash-neutral by month six **only if both invoices are paid and overruns remain contained**.

## 4. Shadow economics: reason not to commercialise at pilot price automatically

Record: development hours, installation hours, safety/legal review, site visits, customer onboarding, training, incident triage, support and rework. Example only:

- 120 unpaid founder hours × illustrative €30/hour = **€3,600 shadow labour**.
- 240 unpaid founder hours × illustrative €30/hour = **€7,200 shadow labour**.

Those noncash amounts would erase the pilot contribution economically, even though the founders spend no wages. For production, replace with loaded salaries, realistic on-call staffing, insurance, amortisation/replacement, procurement, lead generation, compliance and partner/legal costs.

## 5. Pricing beyond the pilot

The integrated-service promise should remain: **do not unbundle the audit and onboarding merely to make a low headline fee look profitable**. Instead, calculate a **single managed-service subscription** from recurring annual cost and amortised installation/startup costs, with clear scope and minimum contract term.

Potential pricing drivers:

```
Required annual per-site revenue
  = sensor replacement / warranty reserve
  + amortised equipment + installation + assessment
  + contracted monitoring / incident-handling hours
  + maintenance and travel
  + hosting and security operations
  + insurance, qualified legal-incident support and compliance overhead allocation
  + customer acquisition & general overhead allocation
  + desired contribution margin
```

**Coverage is not linear in office floor area.** Opaque walls, height, roof geometry, line of sight, lighting and noise produce discontinuous sensor needs. Quote by **risk zone / geometry / required stand-off / operating conditions / response SLA**, not simply by square metre or office floor.

A production price above €1,200/quarter is acceptable per team intent. No definitive market price can be justified until buyer discovery and field scope.

## 6. Go-to-market and buying logic

Probable economic buyer: Head of Security / Chief Security Officer / site industrial security lead, with R&D/IP owner as sponsor; compliance, DPO, IT/OT and legal as gatekeepers. Validate these roles in actual enterprise interviews. Avoid treating every high-R&D company as a qualified lead merely because it has a budget.

For each qualified opportunity, ask: (1) what visible trade secret is at risk, (2) current airspace incidents and actions, (3) exposure duration and consequences, (4) who can approve roof/camera/mic installation, (5) what legal/insurance requirements apply, (6) the value of integrated runbooks versus standalone detectors and existing security vendors.

## 7. Competitive reality

- **Orange Business** publicly launched *Orange Drone Guardian* as a managed counter-drone service in Europe in March 2026, including managed detection and operations.
- **Dedrone** advertises multi-sensor systems, SOP support and forensic evidence workflows.
- **Materna** advertises drone exposure assessments, integration, alert rules and training as part of managed security monitoring.

Therefore the team's differentiation is not first-to-market; it is **R&D trade-secret focus, tailored evidence & action design, sensor-agnostic integration, and an accessible deployment pattern** — all hypotheses to validate against alternatives.

## 8. Funnel and retention metrics

Pilot-qualified conversations; signed access permissions; approved test flight permits; deployment outlay versus cap; number of actionable alerts; user-perceived usefulness; false alerts per week; founder hours; second-quarter renewal/payment; willingness to pay production price; customer reference and case-study permissions.

The future B2B2G/intelligence business is an **option**, not part of the six-month revenue forecast. Cross-site data aggregation must preserve confidentiality and be opt-in/lawful; never sell a claimed surveillance or government-actor attribution capability without evidence.
